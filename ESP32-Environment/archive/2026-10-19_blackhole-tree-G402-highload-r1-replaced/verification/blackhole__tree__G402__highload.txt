 [6] WHY THE BOARD MOVES WERE ALLOWED - OR NOT   (D-23; full note: docs/attack-validation/REROUTE-VERDICT.md)
     The question is not 'did a board change parent?' but 'did the move change WHO the attack reaches?' A
     blackhole only harms traffic that passes through it [KW], and ESP-WIFI-MESH heals itself: when a
     parent breaks down, its children first retry it, then each picks a new parent [E].
     How: for every board, every second, its parents are followed up to the root -> VICTIM (passes the
     attacker), BYSTANDER (does not), or NO PATH (detached / a board above it went down).
     Rules: a board must stay VICTIM or BYSTANDER the whole attack (3 s+ of each = NOT ALLOWED); the
     attacker may never move; NO-PATH seconds are left out of the test; where 60 s+ came before the first
     move, the attack must also be confirmed on those seconds alone.
     Fairness: decided from the layout only - delivery is never used to allow a move [BA]; the limits were
     fixed in advance and are the same for every run [PR].

     This run:
       - B768: NOT ALLOWED - it was a VICTIM for 94 s and a BYSTANDER for 27 s: the attack reached it only
         part of the time, so its attack is two experiments mixed
       - B768: WARNING - no attack windows after +124 s (went silent, or too few samples); the verdict
         uses the windows that exist
       - Left out of the test: 4 attack second(s) in total with no path to the root - they show 0 delivery
         for a reason that is not the attack, so leaving them out can only make the attack HARDER to
         confirm

     So: a move changed who the attack reached (or the re-test failed) - SETUP INVALID.
